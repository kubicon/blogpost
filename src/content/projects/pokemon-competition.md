### November 1, 2026
The Deck encoder in the new model also started collapsing, the weights were growing too much. It seems that it is caused because the gradient flows through the deck encoder from all the decision, so the gradients are pushed in one direction quite a lot and gradually the value was growing too much. Since we can precompute the embedding of all decks before hand, I tried to subtract the mean of this embedding from the trained embeddings during loss computation, such that the encoder will learn only the difference between the decks and not push them all. This seems to fix it, so the encoder did not start creeping up.

Also I ran one of the checkpoints against the v1 model. I was surprised that second day of the training, the version 2 manages to perform as well as v1. I thought this would take at least week. I hope these gains will continue. If I decide to go for version 3, then this is a nice information to have, because I do not need to train the model for month, but week could be enough.

### October 31, 2026
I have decided to finally start training of the second version, such that I will avoid some of the problems from the gradual design I used before. For example the opponent prior from the Bo3 matches was not used much (the RMS was 0.04). I can also fix some of the problems that happened from vibe-coding, like having ReLU in the policy head. I do not know why the Claude used GeLU as I wanted everywhere but this is the only place it used the ReLU.

For version 2, I opted to those changes: Instead of using just deck encoder to encode information about the deck, I will also use token for each card type, which was used by No.1 player in the last competition round. I will add tokens for the opponent prior and deck encoder, such that we hopefully use more information out of it. I will also use GLU as activation, RMS Norm instead of Layer Norm, bfloat16. The model is overall bigger, the embedding dimension for the old was 256 and the transformer used 4 heads with depth 4, now we use 384 for embedding and 6 heads with depth 6. Also we expanded the history slightly, now there are 24 tokens that specify 24 last actions.

For the training I decided to use the v1 model as a teacher, where for 40k iterations, it will trian both actor and critic to be followed with cosine schedule. Surprisingly, because we use bfloat16 and I decided to drop the PPO 2 iterations on the same batch (now I use just one), the speed decreased only roughly by 30% even if the model is much bigger. Also I use imitation with small coefficient to learn even the good actions from the ladder opponents.

### October 25, 2026
The competition is getting more difficult. It seems that some of the top teams have performance better than my method. This forced me to find whether there are not some dead weights or other problems with the training. Few things are correct like Card embedding, or how we use the information about the opponent in future rounds. But surprsingly Deck encoder, which takes all the cards and encodes them to two tokesn completely diverged in two different ways. Both tokens it produced were identical, and they were same regardless of the used deck. This forced me to completely change the encoder and plug it back in. But the same thing was still happening and the LayerNorm was masking this problem. So the second fix added residual connection that bypasses the LayerNorm and it seems that the encoder is now training properly. It was not that important as we stored the information about our deck at several different places, but hopefully this would help to distinguish performance between vastly different decks.

I am still running few imitations daily to make more variable gameplay. My network does not seem to know how to stall, it often tries to play while it can, which is not ideal against some type of opponents. I hope that the imitation is helping to show the network that some of these decisions are still reasonable and it should be less aggressive in a way. 

I did quite a lot imitation on two specific players. The top player, which wins even matchups where he is not favorable and player who is using combo deck. Both of those playstyles are vastly different from my model, so I would like to teach the network to be slightly more aware of those playstyles and mix the into it's play.

For the next submission I will try to use top-3 actions instead of top-2. I think the model has sufficiently trained such that the third action will not be noise, but it may promote this different gameplay I am seeking. Maybe I should also try top-1 again, to see whether it is better.

### October 18, 2026
I tried few different things in the last 2 days. I tried the matrix-game of all decks we use. It was not good idea, because our deck-pool does not really reflect the actual ladder, so our combination was not playing very well against aggresive decks, which are not at the top of the ladder, but they are pretty strong. So I had to try limit one of the players in the game such that it only uses the ladder games. This pushed me into top ~20, but still quite far from the top submissions.

I started relying a bit more on data from ladder than before. I mean that I use the statistics about the decks of top players to estimate which deck should I run. This helps quite a lot, as I orient towards the stronger decks. I mixed across 3 decks, but found out that mixing amongst two decks is sufficient. It is funny that at the ladder different "bands" contain different decks that you would like to counter, so ~900 decks are countered by completely different decks that those at ~1100, which are different form top 3 decks. It is part of the rules, but I feel it's almost cheating when the top decks are never matched to the weaker decks that directly counter them, because now when you try the same deck, even with better network, you can't get near them. Well, that's just the name of the game, it only means we need to do better.

Then I had to fix some observation problems, where for example we couldn't distinguish if we had the same 2 pokemons in the battlefield. Then we couldn't distinguish between them which sucked. This taught me new thing, because usually I would have to restart the training, but after several days, you do not want to do that, but you can initialize the weights such such that they produce identity and do not change the model at all. It probably limits how much you reuse these new features, but just the fact that this works is great, because we do not need to retrain the model from scratch and we can just use the trained checkpoints. 

Also one funny thing happened, I asked Claude whether it can find some obvious tactical mistakes and it was like: Yes of course there are tactical mistakes, and are easily fixable by rule. I added the rule and performance dropped. So I asked again what happened and it was like: Yeah, it is sometimes good not to do this for which we added rule. Another reason why trusting Claude with everythin may be a bad idea.

### October 8, 2026 
Kaggle introduced a challenge where teams should train AI models in Pokemon TCG and then match them against each other. It uses limit pool of cards (roughly 1400) and you are pretty free to do whatever with it. The same competition was already running before, but I did not attend, because I noticed too late, which made me quite sad. Luckily now I caught it at the beginning so I have plenty of time to figure something out. I think I have quite a lot of knowledge about how to train RL to play large games, so I am looking forward to work on this project. My approach would be similar to that of Ataraxos from Sam Sokota (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11036-y), whom I have met couple of times, especially during my time at CMU. I will not just redo the Ataraxos as I do not like some decisions he made. But I have started with creating few default things that you would need to do, including V-trace, PPO, MMD etc. 

For my design I decided to split the training into two phases that will alternate. The first part is a single player problem of constructing the deck, second is multiplayer problem of playing well. So I created some initial implementation and I want to profile it on GPU to see what is the bottleneck. I would like to have everything running on GPU, but I am not sure whether there are substantial gains from that. Of course, this is one thing I want to test. I am not sure how difficult logic the Pokemon TCG performs, I know that logic of Hearthstone, which is also TCG, is very complicated and doing it on GPU in Jax, will probably be even slower than doing it in the C++ provided by the competition authors. Also the limit size for the models is ~200Mb, meaning I cannot train crazy large model just to avoid the bottleneck of the CPU-GPU transfer. Maybe I could ask Claude to redo the logic into CUDA, but I am not sure whether this is a good idea.
